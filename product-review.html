What is EverPitch?

EverPitch is browser-based AI webinar software. You give it a topic, a document, a link, a PDF deck or a recording, and it produces a webinar script, a slide deck and an AI voice-over. Once you have reviewed and edited the result, EverPitch hosts the webinar on a schedule, builds the registration and replay pages, sends reminders and follow-ups, and answers viewers’ questions out loud using your content.

It is sold through JVZoo in two main forms: EverPitch on its own, and the EverPitch Bundle, which adds more credits, done-for-you webinars, ready-made kits and a client licence. This review focuses on the Bundle, since that is the offer this site links to. For a plain product summary without the analysis, see What Is It.

How does EverPitch claim to work?

According to the seller, the process has three steps:

  1. Describe it, or pick a kit. Type the topic, attach a PDF, upload a recording or start from one of 24 kits.
  2. Review it. The talk, slides and voice come back; change any slide by asking and add on-camera clips made from one photo.
  3. Share it. Publish a registration page or embed a join button; EverPitch presents on schedule.

During a session, the talk plays on a shared clock so everyone sees the same moment, and the host answers questions out loud from the webinar’s content. These are the seller’s descriptions. We explain each step in more depth on How It Works.

What independent sources say about the technology

No independent study has tested EverPitch itself. What does exist is government guidance on the technologies it relies on. This guidance describes general risks; it says nothing about EverPitch specifically.

Independent · NIST

Generative AI can produce confident errors

NIST’s Generative AI Profile (AI 600-1) lists confabulation, meaning confidently stated false content, among the risks of generative AI. Practical takeaway: read every line of an AI-written script before it goes live.

View source
Independent · FTC

Voice cloning carries real misuse risks

The FTC has highlighted fraud and misuse of people’s voices as harms of AI voice cloning. EverPitch’s consent confirmation is the right design; it is still your responsibility to get genuine permission.

Independent · FTC

Fake reviews and testimonials are prohibited

The FTC’s rule on consumer reviews and testimonials, in effect since October 21, 2024, bans fake testimonials, including AI-generated ones. This matters if you add social proof to an automated webinar.

AI marketing claims need evidence

FTC staff guidance warns businesses to keep claims about AI products truthful and supported. Apply the same scepticism to any review, including this one, that promises results.

Risks and possible downsides

Software has no physical side effects, but there are practical risks to manage:

  • Accuracy. AI-written scripts and spoken answers can be wrong. Edit the script and add your own FAQ to the host’s knowledge.
  • Honesty about the format. An evergreen webinar is prepared in advance. Don’t imply it is a one-off live event, and keep the simulated-audience option disclosed if you use it.
  • Consent. Only clone a voice, or feature a person, with clear permission.
  • Data and email. You collect registrants’ details and send them email, so privacy and anti-spam rules apply to you.
  • Vendor dependency. Your webinars run on EverPitch’s service. The sales page does not state long-term service or uptime terms, so read the terms of service before relying on it.
  • Upsells. Ultra is sold separately; the Bundle itself is the complete package for everything else listed.

Who may find EverPitch relevant?

  • Coaches, consultants and course creators who repeat the same pitch on calls
  • Service businesses and SaaS teams selling something that needs explaining
  • People who don’t want to appear on camera or present live
  • Agencies building webinar funnels for clients (Agency licence in the Bundle)
  • Sellers reaching audiences in several languages

It is a weaker fit if you need real-time live streaming, run very long webinars every week (credits), or have no plan for getting visitors to register.

Who should get professional advice first?

EverPitch isn’t a health product, so medical advice doesn’t apply. Some users should still check with a qualified professional before relying on it:

  • Businesses in regulated fields such as finance, health, legal or insurance, where presentations and claims are subject to specific rules
  • Agencies signing client contracts that cover branding, data and ownership of content
  • Anyone planning to clone a voice or use another person’s likeness
  • Sellers emailing audiences in the EU, UK or other regions with strict privacy and marketing-consent laws

Final verdict

EverPitch is worth considering if you sell something that needs explaining and you want a webinar working for you every day without scripting, designing or presenting it yourself. On paper the Bundle is strong value at $199.10 one time, and the 30-day refund lowers the risk of trying it.
