After the Webinar: A Follow-Up Email Plan That Respects People

Published November 19, 2026By the everpitch.vercel.app editorial team

Most webinar sign-ups don’t buy during the session, and many don’t attend at all. Follow-up emails are where a lot of the value is recovered, but only if they are useful, well-timed and easy to opt out of.

Segment by what people actually did

  • Stayed to the end: they heard the offer. Send a short recap, answer the main objection, and include the link.
  • Left early: they missed the most important part. Point them to the moment they missed rather than the whole replay.
  • Registered but didn’t attend: a simple “here’s the replay” works better than a guilt trip.
  • Asked a question: answer it personally if you can. It is often the highest-intent group.

Timing that feels helpful

A reasonable rhythm is a replay or recap within a day, a useful follow-up a couple of days later, and a final reminder before any real deadline. If there is no genuine deadline, don’t invent one.

Keep it legal and respectful

In the US, the CAN-SPAM Act applies to commercial email. Among other things, it requires accurate “From” and subject lines, a valid physical postal address, and a clear way to opt out that you honour promptly. The FTC’s compliance guide is in our references. Other countries have their own rules, so check the ones that apply to your list.

One rule of thumb

Write every follow-up as if the reader will reply. If an email would be awkward to send to one person you know, it is probably wrong for your whole list too.
